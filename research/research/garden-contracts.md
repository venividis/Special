## 1. Executive summary

Pixel Garden is an "NFT-owned application environment". One ERC-721 ("Garden") minted from `PixelGardenKernel` gets, atomically in the mint transaction, **two fixed ERC-6551 accounts**: **Reach** (a bounded executor that can withdraw assets and run reviewed "application" calls) and **Grip** (a receive-only vault with no exit path at all). The kernel also owns an **immutable browser host** (a ≤196,608-byte raw HTML archive frozen before the first mint and served by `document(id)` / `tokenURI(id)` as a `data:` URI and `web3://` address), a **content catalog** (content-only cartridge NFTs = archived HTML apps), an **application catalog** (financial/social runtime NFTs admitted by *code hash*, not by address), **append-only state archives** per app namespace, and ownership-transition grants (succession, estate sale, rental pointer, transfer seal).

The unit of extension is a **cartridge**: a separate ERC-721 collection whose contract bytecode hash is pinned in the kernel constructor (or in compiler-generated constants). Ten families exist: 1 Market (Uniswap v3/v4 swaps + ETH/token v4 LP launches), 2 Locks (fixed-beneficiary vesting + beneficiary-authorized maturity sales), 3 Social (rooms, posts, reactions, P-256 encrypted whispers, EIP-712 identity attestation), 4 Continuity (dead-man succession), 5 Estate (visual rentals + inventory-bound Garden sale), 6 Presale (fixed-price ETH presale of an existing token graduating into Market LP), 7 Curve (ascending-price lot sale graduating into Liquidity LP), 8 Liquidity (sorted v4 pairs, v2 swaps, fee sharing), 9 Launchpad (CREATE2 fixed-supply ERC-20 and user-authored Uniswap v4 hook deployment), 10 OTC (named-party bundle escrow with broker fee). Families 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9 are callable from Reach through a single typed `execute(gardenId, Call)` ABI with at most two exact input allowances and two output floors; families 3, 4, 5, 10 have **no** Reach spending policy and authenticate the Garden owner directly.

The storage layer (`ArchiveFactory`, `AppChunk`, `OnchainApp`, `OnchainAppDirectory`) is content-addressed SSTORE2-style bytecode storage adapted from IPSEITY (the `Most-Advanced-NFT-Possible` repo); accounts are "Inspired by IPSEITY-FINAL IpseityAccount/GripVault" (`src/accounts/GardenAccount.sol:7`). The project is a **predeployment development build**: no public chain deployment, no audit; tests run on a disposable Hardhat chain plus mainnet-fork scenarios pinned at block 26,059,508.

Measured latest: 20 production artifacts, all under EIP-170; kernel 23,785 runtime / 45,494 initcode bytes; Market 24,340 runtime (236 bytes of headroom); host 196,543 / 196,608 bytes; `npm run check` = 396 automated tests, zero failures/skips.

## 4. Component-by-component

### 4.1 `src/PixelGardenKernel.sol` — the identity core (812 lines)

**Purpose.** ERC-721 collection "Pixel Garden" / "GARDEN" that (a) derives and creates two ERC-6551 accounts per token, (b) tracks a monotonic `custodyEpoch` per token, (c) holds one frozen host archive, (d) keeps the owner-controlled content and application catalogs with activation epochs, (e) keeps append-only state archives, (f) records permanent LP positions, (g) grants and executes ownership transitions for Continuity/Estate, and (h) renders `tokenURI`.

**Constructor** (`:157-218`): `(uint256 maxSupply_, ContentCartridge contentCartridges_, bytes32 applicationRuntimeHash_ /*Market*/, bytes32 locksRuntimeHash_, bytes32 socialRuntimeHash_, bytes32 continuityRuntimeHash_, bytes32 estateRuntimeHash_, bytes32 presaleRuntimeHash_)`. **Invariants** (enforced, tested):
1. Accounts exist and are verified before any receiver callback; `accountReady` flips only after mint fully succeeds; receiver rejection rolls back NFT, epoch and both account deployments (`accounts.test.mjs` "receiver rejection rolls back…").
2. Every owner-controlled mutation requires the live `custodyEpoch`; every transfer increments it; "transfer-away-and-back" never revives prior activations/sessions/grants.
3. Installation ≠ authority: catalog acceptance grants no spending; `invalidateCartridge` runs before any Reach ERC-721 withdrawal so the outgoing callback sees an inactive installation.
4. A Garden can never be sent to the zero address (no burn), to itself, to its implementations, to its own accounts, or to any deployed canonical account of the collection.
5. Only the exact granted application (by address and family) can move the Garden, and only while the grant's epoch and revision are current; nested transfers during the transition are blocked (`transition == 2`).
6. Content selection: `selectedContent[id][contentKey]` bumps an epoch when the selected release changes, invalidating every sibling edition of the previous release without touching their records ("selection generation prevents sibling resurrection", `state.test.mjs:515`).

**Design decisions and why (quotes).** "Receipt alone is inert. Only the ready Garden's current owner may activate content." (`:330`); "A public display label, not a unique name or ENS ownership claim." (`:336`); "Immutable visual variation only; custody and account identity remain token-ID based." (`:304`); "Only this deployment's reviewed immutable runtime families are admitted." (`:536`). From docs: "An immutable Reach runtime cannot acquire missing Solidity rules after deployment" (ACCOUNT-FOUNDATION.md); "Missing Solidity rules cannot be added later with browser code" (AGENTS.md). ERC-6860 note at `:242-243`: "ERC-6860 auto-mode uses the final string's .html suffix for MIME. The original one-argument recovery endpoint remains unchanged."

**ERCs**: ERC-721 (Solady), ERC-6551 account creation/derivation (registry singleton + ERC-1167 footer), ERC-4804/6860 (`web3://` address emitted in metadata; `document(uint256,string)` auto-mode), ERC-165 via Solady.

**Tests**: `test/accounts.test.mjs` (14 checks: four distinct verified accounts; receipts/withdrawals; Grip rejects all exit selectors; operators have no account authority; transfer/transfer-back epochs; forged/copied/wrong-salt accounts; precreated accounts gain authority only after mint; mint-callback reentry; rollback; withdrawal-callback reentry; self/nested custody rejected; taxed transfers roll back; wrong registry bytecode; immutable visual seeds), `test/cartridges.test.mjs` (host freeze before mint, metadata round-trip, catalog acceptance, removal/return, state append/migrate), `test/state.test.mjs` (migration CAS, sibling invalidation, resumable journals), `test/continuity.test.mjs`, `test/estate.test.mjs` (transition grants, seals), `test/browser-kernel.mjs`.

**Weaknesses / TODO**: 791 bytes of runtime headroom; constructor hard-codes six families plus four compiled constants — any new family is a new kernel; `document()` calls `readAll()` which only `OnchainApp` (schema 1) implements, while `freezeHost` accepts a schema-2 directory via `validateArchive` — freezing a directory host would make `document()`/`tokenURI()` revert forever (one-shot, publisher-only, so low likelihood but a foot-gun); global single `transition`/`minting` locks; no burn; `mint` is free and permissionless up to `maxSupply`; `tokenURI` is huge (base64 of ~196 KB inside a percent-encoded JSON) and relies on generous `eth_call` gas; no multi-chain or ERC-2981.

**Reuse verdict: ADAPT.** The identity/epoch/catalog/host-freeze/document pattern is exactly the "mint a website" mechanism the GOAL needs, and the account-creation-in-mint path is proven. But the fixed ten-family inventory, the Uniswap-specific policy table and the near-cap size argue for re-cutting it: keep `_controller`, epochs, `isCanonicalAccount`, `freezeHost`/`document`, catalog + state, transition grants; drop or generalise the hard-coded family list (e.g. a constructor-time array of admitted hashes with a policy word each) and consider moving catalogs into a facet/satellite if size demands.

## 5. Cross-cutting patterns worth naming

1. **Three-epoch binding.** `custodyEpoch` (kernel, per Garden), `activationEpoch` (per installation), `transferEpoch` (per app/content edition). Every grant, session, membership, plan, offer and listing stores the epochs it was created under and is checked live; nothing is ever "re-enabled" by returning to a previous state.
2. **Code-hash admission, not address allowlists.** `runtimeFamily(address) = f(extcodehash)`; a family hash is the kernel's whole knowledge of a cartridge. Deployer-supplied hashes are checked only for nonzero/distinct; "The fingerprint verifies the family, not the honesty of a publisher, ERC-20 or configured external venue."
3. **Typed call with exact-delta reconciliation on both sides.** Reach approves exactly, calls `execute`, then verifies allowances are zero and balances moved within bounds; the cartridge independently pulls exact amounts, measures protocol deltas, and remits unspent inputs and observed outputs back to `msg.sender` (the Reach). Taxed/rebasing tokens revert by construction.
4. **Commitment handshake between cartridges.** A caller sets `launchContext`/`saleContext = keccak(domain, chainid, kernel, caller, ids, full terms, deadline)` and `executingMarket`; the callee recomputes the commitment from its own arguments and compares; `receive()` on the caller only accepts ETH from `executingMarket` while the context is live; the context is cleared and re-checked after the call.
5. **Receipt-bound safe callbacks.** OTC sets `receipt = keccak(kind, token, from, id, amount)` immediately before a `safeTransferFrom` and `onERC*Received` must match and clear it; all other cartridges accept `onERC721Received` only from the PositionManager while `entered == 2`.
6. **Content-addressed storage with descriptor pinning.** Chunk address = CREATE2(sha256(payload)); archives pin stored sha256 and length on-chain; descriptors carry stored and expanded hash/length plus the reader's codehash; `validateArchive` re-checks all of it; recovery clients must verify expanded bytes themselves.
7. **Pull payments everywhere** (`credit`/`liability`, `feeCredit`/`feeLiability`, `brokerCredit`, `escrowed`), with `_solvent` checks before and after every outgoing transfer so one impaired asset cannot be paid from another deal's backing.
8. **Permissionless gas payers for fixed recipients** (`claim`, `claimRent`, `claimCredit`, `settle`, `refund`, `finalize`, `executeSale`) — "Anyone may pay gas but never redirect."
9. **One-way switches**: `freezeHost`, `openEditions`, `sealAsset/sealAll/sealTransfer`, `retireIdentity`.

## 7. How the web UI is delivered and how ownership is verified (explicit answer)

**Delivery — from chain, with an optional static reader.**
- The host is a single self-contained HTML file (`dist/kernel-host.html`, ≤196,608 bytes) published as a raw `ArchiveFactory` archive and pinned once by `PixelGardenKernel.freezeHost` before the first mint. `document(id)` concatenates the archive's `readAll()` output with `<script>window.PIXEL_CONTEXT={"chainId","contract","tokenId"}</script></body></html>`; `tokenURI(id)` wraps it as `data:application/json,…` with `animation_url = data:text/html;base64,<document>` and `external_url = web3://<kernel>:<chainId>/document/<id>/string!index.html` (ERC-4804/6860). So any ERC-6860 gateway or `web3://` browser renders the app straight from the kernel, and any NFT viewer that honours `animation_url` gets it from metadata. No server, no IPFS.
- Applications (Market UI, Social UI, etc.) are **separate archives** referenced by content releases; the host recovers them from `ArchiveFactory` chunks at a pinned block, verifies stored/expanded sha256 and reader codehash, and runs them in an opaque-origin `sandbox="allow-scripts"` iframe with a CSP that denies network; "trusted" native workspaces additionally must match a build-time sha256 compiled into the host.
- Convenience path: `tools/build-launcher.mjs` produces an HTTPS **reader** (`dist/launcher.html`) that recovers the frozen host from chain and refuses any host whose `expandedHash` differs from the build's pin (`sdk/launcher.mjs:78-105` "This reader does not recognize the frozen host build"); it "never executes metadata's `animation_url`". This is a static site, but it only bootstraps bytes that are verified against the chain. The dev server (`npm run dev`) serves `dist/index.html` = `document(1)` plus a dev-only context block.

**Ownership verification — client-side gate, contract-side enforcement.**
- `web/kernel/client.mjs:109-150`: on `initialize()` the client checks `provider.getNetwork().chainId == PIXEL_CONTEXT.chainId` and `kernel.deploymentChainId()`; `refresh()` reads `ownerOf(tokenId)`, `custodyEpoch`, `account`, `grip`, `accountReady`, `displayName`, `visualSeed` and throws "Account binding failed" unless `isCanonicalAccount` holds for both accounts.
- `connect()` (`:159-185`) uses EIP-1193 (`eth_requestAccounts`, or `eth_accounts` for silent reconnect; EIP-6963 discovery in the launcher), requires the wallet chain to match, then `owns = account.toLowerCase() === owner.toLowerCase()` (`:152`). The header shows "OWNER CONNECTED" vs "PUBLIC VIEW · EPOCH n" (`web/kernel/host.mjs:221`); every owner control is `disabled` when `!owns`; `client.send()` with `ownerRequired` throws "Only the current Garden owner can do this" (`client.mjs:212`) and re-checks owner and epoch again immediately before the wallet prompt (`:228`). Account/chain changes clear signing state.
- There is **no login signature and no server session**: authorisation is simply that the connected EOA is `ownerOf(tokenId)` (or holds a Reach session key / is the named beneficiary, party, heir, renter, etc. for the respective cartridge). The contracts are the real gate: `_controller` (owner + live epoch), `ReachAccount.authorized` (owner + epoch), `Social._auth`, `Estate._owner`, `OTC.propose` (owner + epoch + active edition) — ERC-721 operators are deliberately excluded everywhere.
- For third parties that want proof of Garden ownership without a transaction, `SocialCartridge.identityDigest/verifyIdentity` provides an EIP-712 "Identity" attestation bound to kernel, Garden, custody epoch, purpose, challenge and deadline, with owner-retirable digests (`social.test.mjs` "typed identity is domain-separated, expiry checked and invalidated by custody transfer").

Caveat recorded in MINT-TO-APP.md: native `web3://` clients still need wallet injection, origin storage, Web Locks and `DecompressionStream`; the HTTPS reader is "the tested compatibility route", and "this is not independent Ethereum consensus/proof verification".

## 8. Known weaknesses, TODOs and risks

- **Size.** Kernel 791 B and Market 236 B under EIP-170; kernel initcode 45,494/49,152. The repo's own rule: "If the kernel exceeds normal limits, move pure validation/rendering into an already inventoried immutable component… never remove authorization, accounting or callback rules to fit" (ESTATE-DESIGN.md:36).
- **Fixed family inventory.** Six constructor hashes + four compile-time constants; a new family means a new kernel and a new host freeze. Already-minted Gardens cannot adopt new families.
- **Venue coupling.** Swaps/launches require Uniswap v3 SwapRouter02, v4 PoolManager/PositionManager/StateView, Permit2 and WETH at deployment (config/ethereum.json, mainnet only); no per-token AMM of its own.
- **Directory host foot-gun.** `freezeHost` accepts schema-2 archives but `document()` needs `readAll()`, which `OnchainAppDirectory` lacks.
- **Storage-heavy social.** Post bodies, whisper envelopes and mailbox indexes in contract storage; unbounded arrays (`members`, `mail`, `posts`).
- **No ERC-1271 / 4337 / 2981 / burn / royalties; no chain-portability** (`deploymentChainId` pinned; registry must exist with exact codehash).
- **Sessions are address-keyed** (an agent must send from the key address), no signature-based delegation.
- **Mixed error styles**: `OnchainApp`, `OnchainAppDirectory`, `LaunchToken` use `require` strings.
- **Tight coupling**: `EstateCartridge` imports the concrete `PixelGardenKernel`/`ReachAccount`.
- **Metadata size**: `tokenURI` returns ~262 KB; the comment on gas caps shows this was already at the edge.
- **Unaudited; predeployment.** Every doc repeats "No public-chain deployment… not an independent financial security audit."
- Evidence numbers drift between checkpoints (e.g. kernel gas 6.85M vs 9.43M; Social 15,853 vs 16,297 bytes) — re-measure before quoting.

## 10. Unique ideas to carry forward

1. Two canonical ERC-6551 accounts per NFT with codehash-verified identity; Grip's security = absence of code.
2. Epoch triple (custody / activation / edition transfer) that makes "transfer away and back" harmless.
3. Code-hash family admission with a kernel-side family→action policy table; cartridges are NFTs you hold in your own account.
4. Single typed `execute(gardenId, Call)` ABI with ≤2 exact inputs/outputs, exact-delta checks on both sides, allowance zeroing, and "exact-recipe" session keys with use counts.
5. Ratcheting seals (asset/global/transfer) as one-way owner commitments.
6. Commitment handshake (`launchContext`/`saleContext`) between cooperating immutable cartridges.
7. Host freeze before first mint + `document()` appending `PIXEL_CONTEXT` + percent-encoded JSON data URI to avoid double base64.
8. Content-addressed chunk dedupe, directory of leaves, descriptor validation, gzip-in-archive with client-side verified expansion.
9. Append-only state namespaces keyed by (collection, publisher, appKey, schema) with CAS revisions and atomic migration/selection epochs.
10. Social: on-chain P-256 key registry per custody epoch, epoch-scoped blocks, EIP-712 identity attestation with retirement, Commons room 0.
11. Inventory-digest NFT sale (Estate) — buyer gets exactly the catalog/seal/balance state they reviewed.
12. OTC receipt-bound safe-transfer callbacks and per-side independent refunds.
13. Presale incremental-floor allocation; Curve closed-form cumulative cost; Liquidity fee share with remainder carry and fee-first zero-liquidity decrease.
14. CREATE2 hook deployment with on-chain flag/address verification and per-Garden nonces.

## 11. Pitfalls for the design team

- Family hashes depend on exact compiler settings (`0.8.36`, runs 1, viaIR, cancun, `bytecodeHash none`); any whitespace change re-keys a family.
- Target chains must host the ERC-6551 registry at `0x0000…5758` with codehash `0xda1d…6735`, Permit2, WETH and the Uniswap deployments; everything is `deploymentChainId`-pinned.
- Permit2 expiry 0 normalises to `block.timestamp` — cleanup must check `expiry ≤ now`, not `== 0`.
- v4 action bytes (`0x02 MINT_POSITION, 0x0d SETTLE_PAIR, 0x14 SWEEP, 0x01 DECREASE_LIQUIDITY, 0x11 TAKE_PAIR`) and the limit-price constants are hard-coded; verify against the deployed PositionManager version.
- `tokenURI` is percent-encoded JSON, **not** base64; consumers must branch on the data-URI encoding.
- Keep `freezeHost` to schema-1 archives unless `readAll` is added to directories.
- Hardhat here is only a chain; compilation goes through `tools/compile.mjs` (solc-js), and tests compile `test/contracts` too (`compile({tests:true})`).
- Don't trust evidence numbers across checkpoints; re-run `npm run compile` to get `out/contract-sizes.json`.
